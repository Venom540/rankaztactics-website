Average cost-per-click on Amazon is sitting around $0.91 this year, and up 15 to 30% year over year in some categories. At those prices, a single flat bid across every keyword and placement isn't a strategy — it's a way of overpaying consistently. Amazon's auction now evaluates conversion probability at the impression level using behavioral signals, placement, and machine learning, which means static bids fall behind fast. Here's what's actually moving the needle for accounts we manage in 2026.

## 1. Use dynamic bidding, not fixed bids

Amazon's "dynamic bids, up and down" setting can adjust your bid by as much as 100% in either direction based on real-time conversion likelihood. It's not perfect, but it consistently outperforms a fixed bid sitting still all day.

## 2. Set placement modifiers deliberately

Top-of-search placements can carry bid adjustments up to 900% above your base bid, and for good reason — that real estate converts very differently from product-page placements. Don't apply one blanket modifier everywhere. Check placement-level ACoS separately and set the multiplier to match what each spot is actually worth to you.

## 3. Daypart your top keywords

Conversion rates vary meaningfully by hour. Raise bids during your proven peak conversion windows and pull back during dead hours. You don't need Amazon Marketing Stream and an AWS setup to start — a few weeks of hourly performance data from your campaign manager is enough to spot the pattern.

## 4. Run auto and manual campaigns in parallel, always

Auto campaigns are your discovery engine — they surface search terms you haven't thought of. Manual exact-match campaigns are where you scale the terms that already convert. The winning move is a standing weekly habit: pull the Search Term Report from auto campaigns, harvest anything converting well, and migrate it into a manual exact-match campaign with its own controlled bid.

## 5. Mine negatives as hard as you mine keywords

Every non-converting search term you don't block is a bid you're paying with no chance of a return. Add exact-match negatives for specific high-cost, zero-conversion terms, and phrase-match negatives to block broader irrelevant traffic. This alone tends to be the single biggest lever for lowering ACoS without touching a single keyword bid.

## 6. Set a target ACoS or target ROAS on stable campaigns

Once a campaign has enough data (generally 2 to 4 weeks of consistent performance), Amazon's automated bidding can hold a target more precisely — and more often — than a human checking in once a week. Keep reviewing weekly, but let the algorithm handle the hour-to-hour adjustments.

## 7. Write your listing content with Amazon's AI shopping assistant in mind

Bidding on "running shoes" still matters, but a growing share of queries now arrive as full sentences ("running shoes good for flat feet under $100"), and how your bullets and A+ Content answer that kind of question increasingly affects whether your ad is even a good match to show. Treat listing copy and PPC targeting as one system, not two separate jobs.

---

**On cadence:** review active campaigns weekly, stable ones every two weeks, and check daily during Q4 or any major sale event. Segment your campaign structure from day one — branded terms, competitor terms, and generic category terms each behave differently and deserve their own budget and bid logic rather than living in one catch-all campaign.

None of this replaces a solid listing. If your main image, title, and price aren't already converting well, better bids just get you more expensive clicks on a page that wasn't going to close the sale anyway. Fix the listing first, then let the bid tactics compound on top of it.
