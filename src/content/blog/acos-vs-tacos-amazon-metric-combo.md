Every Amazon seller learns to watch ACoS early. It's the first ad metric Seller Central puts in front of you, and it's simple to read: spend $20 on ads, make $100 in ad sales, and your ACoS is 20%. Lower is better, higher is worse, done.

Except it's not done. ACoS on its own has talked more sellers into bad decisions than almost any other number on the platform. The fix isn't a better ACoS. It's tracking a second metric alongside it: TACoS, or Total Advertising Cost of Sales.

## What each one actually measures

**ACoS** = Ad Spend divided by Ad-Attributed Sales, times 100. It only looks at sales your ads can take direct credit for. It tells you how efficient one specific campaign or keyword is, in isolation, right now.

**TACoS** = Ad Spend divided by Total Sales (ad sales plus organic sales), times 100. It puts that same ad spend against your entire business, not just the slice your ads touched. It tells you how dependent your revenue is on paid traffic, and whether that dependency is rising or falling over time.

Same numerator, different denominator, completely different question answered.

## Two listings, identical ACoS, opposite businesses

Say you manage a listing with $10,000 a month in total sales, and $2,000 of ad spend generated $4,000 of that in ad sales. Your ACoS is 50%. On its own, that number looks alarming — most sellers start trimming bids somewhere around 35 to 40%.

Now check TACoS: $2,000 divided by $10,000 is 20%. Only a fifth of total revenue is being rented through ads. The other 80% is organic, which means the ads are doing their real job: building enough visibility and velocity that Amazon's own algorithm is sending free traffic on top of it.

Flip it. A different listing also shows a 50% ACoS, but total sales are only $4,200. TACoS here is roughly 48% — almost identical to ACoS. Almost nothing is happening organically. Turn the ads off and the business mostly disappears with them.

Same ACoS. Completely different businesses. If you only ever look at ACoS, you'd treat these two listings the same way, and you shouldn't.

## A quick gut check

Ask what would happen if every campaign paused tomorrow. If total sales would fall by more than 50 to 60%, there's a paid-traffic dependency problem, no matter how good the ACoS looks on a report. If sales would mostly hold up, the ads are doing exactly what they're supposed to: building momentum that outlives the click.

## Rough TACoS benchmarks by stage

- **Launch (months 0 to 6):** 25 to 50% is normal. You're buying ranking signals and reviews you don't have yet.
- **Growth (months 6 to 18):** 15 to 25%, and it should be trending down month over month as organic picks up the slack.
- **Mature listings (18 months plus):** 5 to 15%, with organic sales making up the majority, often 60 to 70% of total revenue.

If TACoS is flat or climbing well past the launch phase, that's the real warning sign — not a slightly elevated ACoS on one campaign.

## The mistake I see most

A seller gets nervous about a 35% ACoS, pulls back spend or pauses a broad match campaign that looked "inefficient." ACoS improves the next week. Except total revenue also drops, because that campaign was quietly feeding organic rank and catching top-of-funnel searches that were never going to convert on the first click anyway.

ACoS improved. The business got smaller. That's the trap.

The fix isn't to ignore ACoS — it still catches genuinely wasteful spend at the keyword level. It's to let TACoS decide whether you're even allowed to be nervous in the first place. Judge ACoS on a rolling 14-day window at minimum; a single bad day means nothing. Judge TACoS monthly, and read the trend, not the number. And before scaling ad spend up, check that TACoS has held steady for at least two consecutive weeks. If it's still climbing, more budget usually just speeds up the wrong trend.
