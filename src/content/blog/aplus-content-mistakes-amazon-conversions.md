A+ Content is free for anyone in Brand Registry, and it's the single largest conversion lever you can pull without touching your price, title, or main images. Amazon's own published benchmark is up to a 10% sales lift. The real number is category-dependent and worth measuring per SKU over at least 30 days, but the ceiling is real.

The problem is that most sellers leave a meaningful chunk of that lift on the table through a small set of repeat mistakes. Here are the nine I see most often, in order of how much damage they do.

## 1. Treating it as a keyword surface

A+ Content text is **not indexed** for Amazon search ranking. Your title, bullet points, backend search terms, and structured attributes are what drive discoverability — A+ is a conversion tool that lives below all of that. Stuffing keywords into it doesn't help you rank, and it makes the copy worse for the one job A+ actually has: convincing someone who already found you to buy.

## 2. Getting the image specs wrong

This is the single most common reason submissions get rejected. Safe defaults: header image 970 × 600px, full-width lifestyle image 970 × 300px, image-and-text module image side around 300 × 300px. Low resolution, wrong aspect ratio, or text baked into the image itself will all get flagged.

## 3. Ignoring mobile entirely

Somewhere around 60 to 70% of Amazon shopping happens on a phone. Amazon's preview tool defaults to desktop, so you have to manually switch to the mobile preview and check every module. Text that's fine on desktop often turns into an unreadable wall on a 6-inch screen.

## 4. Leaving the plain-text description in place

A+ Content is meant to replace your standard product description, not sit alongside it. If your listing still has the old plain description doing double duty somewhere, that's wasted space.

## 5. Listing features instead of selling outcomes

Customers don't care that a tent uses 300D Oxford nylon — they care that it stays dry in a storm. Every module should translate a spec into a real-world benefit before it's allowed onto the page.

## 6. Overloading the header module

The first module is what decides whether someone keeps scrolling. Cramming in every badge, claim, and headline you can think of usually backfires — one clear product promise beats five competing ones.

## 7. Writing paragraphs instead of scannable copy

If a module runs past 3 to 4 sentences, most shoppers have already stopped reading. Treat each module like a page in a magazine, not a page in a manual.

## 8. Using generic stock-style lifestyle photography

The "happy family in a sunny field" or "person smiling while holding a coffee mug" shots get tuned out immediately because shoppers have seen the exact same photo on ten other listings. Real photography of your actual product, in an actual use case, does more work.

## 9. Skipping the comparison chart — or only using it for size and color variants

A well-built comparison module is one of the highest-converting things you can add, and it's also a quiet cross-sell opportunity if you show your other products alongside variants, not just the one someone's currently looking at.

---

A few other things worth keeping in mind: don't mention pricing, shipping speed, warranties, or competitor names anywhere in A+ Content — these get flagged as promotional or prohibited content and are one of the most common rejection reasons alongside bad image specs.

And if you've published 5 approved Standard A+ submissions in the past 12 months, check whether **Premium A+** has unlocked for you automatically. Plenty of sellers qualify and never notice, which means they're leaving video, comparison hotspots, and Q&A modules unused for no reason.

Last thing: treat A+ Content as something you test, not something you publish once and forget. Amazon's Manage Your Experiments tool lets Brand Registry sellers A/B test different versions directly, and it's worth running at least once on your top SKUs before assuming your first draft was the right one.
